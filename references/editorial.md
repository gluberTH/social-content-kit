# Editorial and evidence decisions

## Source selection

Build a compact matrix: source URL, title, publication/update date if available, topic, audience value, evidence, decision, and caveat. Prefer a source that answers a concrete question over one chosen only because it is newest. Record analytics only when actually available and relevant; state the observation window.

Separate verified product facts, editorial advice, and illustrative examples. Product facts need current evidence. Advice must not imply a product capability. Fictional scenarios, amounts, names, interfaces, and dates need clear labels.

Do not invent testimonials, customer quotes, results, growth, time savings, user counts, rankings, credentials, availability, discounts, guarantees, or research. If a public page makes a claim that is not adequately supported, omit or qualify it instead of copying it uncritically.

## Thin-content sites

A small blog can still support a useful campaign. Build topics around a recurring question, a decision, a checklist, a misconception, a workflow, an honest limitation, or a product walkthrough. Link to an existing page that fulfills the promise. Do not say "read the guide" when the destination is only a homepage, or "download" when there is no download.

## Tone and platform adaptation

Use plain, concrete language. Avoid generic praise, manufactured urgency, fear-based selling, and repeated "game-changing" claims. Preserve the user's supplied style when suitable for the new audience, but do not invent first-person founder anecdotes.

Write a distinct angle for each network. Reuse a theme across networks when useful, not necessarily its whole caption or the same image. A product explanation should show what the audience can do and what remains their responsibility.

For trial, pricing, and subscription posts, verify duration, start trigger, card requirement, auto-renewal/charge behavior, limits, currency, taxes, and material conditions. Include the conditions needed to make the post accurate; link to fuller details. Do not turn a trial into a free-forever promise.

## Measurement

Use one campaign name and a unique content ID per post when adding UTM. Preserve existing functional query parameters and anchors. Do not replace signed or payment URLs with tracking variants without understanding their contract.

A results tracker should begin blank. Keep impressions, outbound clicks, saves, replies, visits, accepted signup requests, verified accounts, and paid conversions separate. Compare equal observation windows. Do not call an accepted form request a completed purchase or verified registration. Note when consent, missing analytics, or attribution gaps affect measurement.

Publishing time is a test hypothesis until supported by audience-specific data. Use timezone-aware conversion, including dates crossing midnight and DST. Rest days and a review date should be visible in the plan.
